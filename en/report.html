Research question 1 · Research brief

Health and care in later life

KLoSA estimates for Korea in 2018 and 2024, with a 2018 CHARLS comparison framework

Methods and data

Who does KLoSA follow?

The Korean Longitudinal Study of Ageing (KLoSA), run by the Korea Employment Information Service, interviews the same people every two years about health, family, work and income. In 2006 it enrolled 10,254 adults aged 45 or older living in private households outside Jeju. In 2014, 920 additional respondents, chiefly people born in 1962–1963, took part in the main interview.

Who can the estimates represent?

KLoSA is a probability sample. It stratified census districts by region, urban or rural location and housing type, then sampled households in 1,000 districts. Weighted estimates can describe adults aged 65+ within the population covered by this design. Individual cross-sectional weights adjust for selection probabilities and nonresponse and align the sample with population totals. Island and institutional districts were outside the original frame. Generalisation is limited for these populations, particularly for people with intensive care needs living in institutions.

KLoSA overview · Official sample design · 2022 KLoSA report, pp. 5–15

Sex and age profile of the analytic sample

Each analysis includes living respondents aged 65+ with a positive individual cross-sectional weight for that wave. The samples overlap, and differences between years do not measure individual decline or recovery. Health-related attrition and item nonresponse may still affect estimates after weighting. Results are descriptive, without age or sex standardisation or design-based confidence intervals.

Disability & function

In 2024, 8.68% reported a history of physician-assessed disability and 7.06% needed help with at least one basic activity. The care chapter examines the overlap. We linked responses from each person’s first interview because people who previously reported an assessment skip that question in later waves.

Basic activities of daily living (ADLs) measure personal care. Respondents describe the past week. Temporary help expected to resolve within three months counts as independence. Partial or full help from another person counts as a limitation.

Questionnaire and release IDs can differ. Distance vision, near vision and hearing are C084, C085 and C091 in the 2024 questionnaire (pp. 89–90) and w10C076, w10C077 and w10C083 in the structured data.

Published 2022 benchmark

Among all 6,057 respondents in 2022, 3.8% needed help with at least one of seven basic activities and 11.6% with at least one of ten instrumental activities. Our estimates for adults aged 65+ use four activities in each domain. Changes over time cannot be inferred across these age ranges and item sets.

Chronic conditions

In 2024, 56.27% reported hypertension, 28.35% arthritis or rheumatism, and 27.69% diabetes or high blood sugar. The share with at least two conditions was 45.18% in 2018 and 45.79% in 2024. Nine respondents in 2018 lacked a valid response for at least one condition and were excluded from the count.

A chronic condition persists over time or requires ongoing management. This analysis uses reported medical diagnoses in eight physical disease categories. Each category counts once. A person with both hypertension and diabetes has a count of two and meets our definition of multimorbidity.

Cognitive function

The weighted 2024 K-MMSE-2 mean was 24.44 out of 30. The median was 26, and the middle half scored 22–29. Among 4,106 people reporting no assistance during testing, the mean was 24.94. The current release does not provide the 2018 test scores. Dementia and mild cognitive impairment diagnoses come from separate survey questions.

The Korean Mini-Mental State Examination, 2nd edition (K-MMSE-2), is a cognitive screening test. Higher raw scores on the 0–30 scale indicate better task performance. Individual screening interpretation uses age and education norms. We report the raw score distribution without assigning a clinical category at a single cutoff. Diagnosis histories come from a separate question.

2024 questionnaire · p. 85, Cadd_01, and pp. 122–125, C400–C421 · K-MMSE-2 user manual, pp. 34–39

The test requires a self-interview. The interviewer records assistance separately. Of 4,504 eligible respondents in 2024, 4,491 had a total score. Of these, 4,106 received no help, 312 received occasional help and 73 received help most of the time. Differences between the full and unassisted means reflect the composition of the groups.

Long-term care needs

In 2024, 902 respondents needed help. Of these, 413 needed basic help and 489 needed instrumental help only (7.06% and 9.86% of all adults aged 65+, weighted). Among the 902, 204 reported no helper (25.98% weighted).

The eight common items cover basic self-care (dressing, bathing, eating, toileting) and instrumental activities of daily living (meals, shopping, finances, medication). Help with any item indicates functional care need. Separate questions cover insurance eligibility and use.

2024 questionnaire · pp. 104–107, C201–C220, and p. 112, C318–C325

People needing help on any of the 17 Korean items can name up to five helpers, including relatives, volunteers and paid carers. Our no-helper estimate uses the eight-item need group. The roster does not assess whether help with each task was adequate.

Sample overlap and care needs by income

Of 4,352 respondents aged 65+ in 2018, 3,063 (70.4%) also participated in 2024. Among the 4,504 respondents aged 65+ in 2024, 1,286 were interviewed below age 65 in 2018 and 155 had no 2018 main interview. The 2024 wave gives a recent Korean profile. The 2018 wave aligns with the Chinese CHARLS survey year.

In 2024, 25.4% of the lowest income quartile and 12.8% of the highest needed help with at least one of eight activities. The corresponding shares were 12.6% and 9.2% at ages 65–74, and 22.6% and 20.0% at ages 75–84. At age 85+ the ordering reversed (50.2% and 62.1%), with 71 valid respondents in the highest quartile. The overall difference does not apply uniformly across ages or establish an income effect.

Among the 3,063 people observed at both endpoints, 570 needed help with a common activity only in 2024 and 67 only in 2018. These are unweighted counts.

Comparability with CHARLS in China

Disability & function Map partial or full assistance in Korea to “need help” or “cannot do” in China. Difficulty while still able to act independently counts as independent. Apply the Chinese questionnaire’s routing rules for skipped basic activities. Korean physician assessment and Chinese self-reported disability types measure different things.

Chronic conditions Use Chinese diagnosis indicators that incorporate earlier reports. In Korea, suspected stroke and transient ischaemic attacks are excluded from confirmed cerebrovascular disease. The definition of each condition and access to diagnosis may affect the comparison.

Cognitive function Orientation and serial subtraction are the first candidates. The Chinese r4orient is scored out of four. Add r4ssn to match the five-item Korean score including season. For three-word memory, use the corresponding raw task rather than the ten-word measure. Repetition, delay, education, language and survey year affect interpretation.

Long-term care needs Meal preparation, shopping, finances and medication need checks for wording, household roles and opportunities to perform the task. Helper status covers activities collectively and cannot establish whether help was adequate for a specific task. Korean long-term care insurance use describes a country-specific programme.
