Methods and data

Who does KLoSA follow?

The Korean Longitudinal Study of Ageing (KLoSA), run by the Korea Employment Information Service, interviews the same people every two years about health, family, work and income. In 2006 it enrolled 10,254 adults aged 45 or older living in private households outside Jeju. In 2014, 920 additional respondents, chiefly people born in 1962–1963, took part in the main interview.

Who can the estimates represent?

KLoSA is a probability sample. It stratified census districts by region, urban or rural location and housing type, then sampled households in 1,000 districts. Weighted estimates can describe adults aged 65+ within the population covered by this design. Individual cross-sectional weights adjust for selection probabilities and nonresponse and align the sample with population totals. Island and institutional districts were outside the original frame. Generalisation is limited for these populations, particularly for people with intensive care needs living in institutions.

KLoSA overview · Official sample design · 2022 KLoSA report, pp. 5–15

Sex and age profile of the analytic sample

Each analysis includes living respondents aged 65+ with a positive individual cross-sectional weight for that wave. The samples overlap, and differences between years do not measure individual decline or recovery. Health-related attrition and item nonresponse may still affect estimates after weighting. Results are descriptive, without age or sex standardisation or design-based confidence intervals.

Who is included?

The samples include KLoSA respondents aged 65 or older with a positive individual cross-sectional weight: 4,352 in 2018 (wave 7) and 4,504 in 2024 (wave 10).

How should I read estimates and counts?

Percentages, means and quartiles use the integrated individual cross-sectional weight for the relevant wave. Counts are observed respondents. Denominators are specific to each indicator. Composite measures require valid responses to every component.

What can the year and country comparisons tell us?

Each year is a separate cross-sectional description. We have not estimated individual change, age- or sex-standardised differences, or survey-design confidence intervals. For a 2018 country comparison, residential coverage, item routing and weights must be checked and a common standard population applied when Chinese data are available.

How were the results checked?

Selected structured-file variables were cross-checked between the SAS and Excel releases for waves 7 and 10. Disability assessment history was linked across waves 1–10. In 2024, the item scores summed to the released cognitive total for every valid respondent.
