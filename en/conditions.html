What the count includes

In 2024, 56.27% reported hypertension, 28.35% arthritis or rheumatism, and 27.69% diabetes or high blood sugar. The share with at least two conditions was 45.18% in 2018 and 45.79% in 2024. Nine respondents in 2018 lacked a valid response for at least one condition and were excluded from the count.

How this is measured

A chronic condition persists over time or requires ongoing management. This analysis uses reported medical diagnoses in eight physical disease categories. Each category counts once. A person with both hypertension and diabetes has a count of two and meets our definition of multimorbidity.

Published 2022 benchmark

In Table 4-6, 21.1% of all 6,057 respondents had two conditions and 17.6% had three or more, giving 38.7% with at least two. That table excludes prostate disease and urinary incontinence. The trend table (4-5) specifies exclusion of prostate disease and reports 41.3%. We cite Table 4-6 for context. Our estimate is restricted to eight physical conditions in adults aged 65+.

Mapping to China

Use Chinese diagnosis indicators that incorporate earlier reports. In Korea, suspected stroke and transient ischaemic attacks are excluded from confirmed cerebrovascular disease. The definition of each condition and access to diagnosis may affect the comparison.
